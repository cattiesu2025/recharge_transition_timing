# Single-sortie work-to-return protocol (v0.3.0 pilot; not formally frozen)

This is a new task revision. Results and checkpoints produced under v0.2.4's recharge-and-resume task are historical pilot evidence and must not be pooled with this revision.

The environment extends MiniGrid 2.5.0. A fully observed 17 × 9 grid has one work tile `(1, 4)` and one terminal home dock `(distance + 1, 4)`, joined by a single traversable corridor. The agent starts at work, facing the dock. One action is one time step. Actions are LEFT, RIGHT, FORWARD, WAIT, and WORK; no charging action or in-episode energy replenishment exists. Invalid actions consume their usual energy and one time step. Work is possible only at `(1, 4)` and only while `remaining > 0`; `quota` is the maximum available work, not a required amount. The agent may depart early. Reaching zero remaining does not end the episode: the agent must travel to the dock.

Arriving at the dock ends the episode. Arrival with positive battery and at least one completed work unit is `returned`; arrival with positive battery and zero work is `returned_without_work`. Battery reaching zero, including on the dock arrival step, is `exhausted` and takes precedence. The 300-step limit is `time_limit` if no terminal outcome occurred. The dock cannot be exited after arrival. The observation includes position, direction, battery, remaining available work, remaining time, corridor distance, and nonnegative dock margin. `E_min_to_dock` includes moves and necessary turns; margin is `battery − E_min_to_dock`.

The per-step pilot reward is `w_P × work_completed − w_R × clip((safe_margin − margin)/capacity, 0, 1) − time_cost`. Shared terminal terms add `return_bonus` for `returned`, subtract `empty_return_penalty` for `returned_without_work`, and subtract `exhaustion_penalty` for `exhausted`. RES/BAL/PROD differ only in `(w_P, w_R)`. Work has immediate reward; completed work and safe returned work are separately reported. All reward values are pilot candidates, not formal frozen values. No threshold forces a return.

The primary ONSET candidate is an actual move from `(1, 4)` to `(2, 4)`. It is confirmed by at least three moves toward the dock within eight steps including entry, before reverse motion, return to work, exhaustion, or episode end. The onset is the entry step and confirmation is the third progress step; docking is not necessary. All candidates and rejection reasons remain recorded. Auxiliary first movement, departure and dock steps, plus sensitivity detectors with two and four progress moves, are reported. An unconfirmed departure is not silently upgraded to a confirmed ONSET merely because the dock was reached.

Development and held-out grids each have 27 matched scenarios. Development crosses distances 4/6/8, batteries 16/19/22, and work caps 6/8/10; held-out crosses distances 5/7/9, batteries 17/20/23, and caps 7/9/11. In every original scenario, at least one WORK followed by the direct route returns with at least `safe_margin`, while completing the entire cap and retaining that margin is impossible. The sufficient-battery intervention sets initial battery to 60 with the same model, geometry and cap; finishing the cap and returning with `safe_margin` is feasible. These inequalities are checked mechanically. Training samples both battery regimes. Reference-controller probes establish feasibility only.

Pilot uses three declared seeds and 300,000 actions per reward condition, with fixed-budget final checkpoints. Use separate v0.3.0 output paths. Formal work needs a separate reviewed `formal` config, 20 declared seeds and an immutable freeze before training; held-out evaluation occurs after freeze. Retain checkpoint/config hashes, every episode row, candidates and compressed trajectories. Old checkpoint hashes cannot authorize evaluation under the new config.

R1 compares matched original scenarios with confirmed return in both RES and PROD. Each seed contributes the median of `PROD onset − RES onset`; the candidate minimum remains 18/27 joint events per seed. A formal estimate requires all 20 declared seeds, complete matched rows, no duplicate/extra/technical-error rows, and formal phase. Use a seed-median estimate, 10,000 seed bootstrap draws and an exact sign test. Positive differences support the proposed later PROD return, subject to work and safety quality. Report event and physical dock rates, work completed, cap completion, empty returns, exhaustion, and dock margin alongside timing. Missing ONSET is never assigned infinite time. `returned_without_work`, exhaustion before/after confirmed ONSET and time limit remain separate outcomes. In the sufficient-battery group, later return and more work are possible; safe return is still required.
