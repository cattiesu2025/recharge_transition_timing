# Recharge return protocol (pilot, not frozen formal analysis)

The task extends MiniGrid 2.5.0 with a fully observable 17 × 9 grid and a single horizontal traversable corridor. The work tile is `(1, 4)`; the charge-only branch is `(2, 4)` through the dock at `(distance + 1, 4)`. MiniGrid supplies the grid, walls, floor tiles, and rendering. Battery, work, charge, reward, atomic action order, full-state encoding, and return events are defined in this project. There are no stochastic objects or partial observations.

An action step is the only time unit. The first policy action starts at `t0=0` in the work tile, facing east. The agent sees position, heading, battery, remaining quota, remaining time, charge streak, corridor distance, and nonnegative dock margin. Actions are turn left/right, move forward, wait, work, and charge. Invalid actions consume their specified energy and one time step. Work is credited only at the work tile; charge is possible only at the dock. Energy is paid before an action takes effect if affordable. An action that cannot be paid has no effect and ends in exhaustion. Arriving at the dock with too little energy for the following charge action also ends in exhaustion. If the final work action leaves zero energy, exhaustion takes precedence over quota completion. Completing quota, exhausting energy, and reaching the 300-step horizon are the only episode endpoints; docking and charging do not end an episode.

`E_min_to_dock` counts forward costs plus the least costly required turn from the current heading. The reserve margin is `battery − E_min_to_dock`. Reward conditions change only the production and reserve weights. Common completion, exhaustion, and time terms are identical. There is no event, docking, or charging bonus.

The primary candidate is an actual move from `(1, 4)` to `(2, 4)`. It is confirmed when three forward moves toward the dock occur within eight action steps including entry, before reverse motion, return to work, exhaustion, or episode end. The onset is the branch-entry step; confirmation is the third progress step. Docking is not required. Every candidate and its rejection reason is retained. Auxiliary first movement, branch exit, dock, and charge steps are recorded. Sensitivity detectors use two and four progress moves on the same trajectory.

Development and held-out grids each contain 27 matched scenarios. Development uses distance 4/6/8, battery 16/18/20, and quota 6/8/10. Held-out uses distance 5/7/9, battery 17/19/21, and quota 7/9/11. The sufficient-battery intervention sets initial energy to 60 without changing model, geometry, or quota. In every original grid cell, the energy required to finish work alone exceeds initial battery; after the intervention, work can finish while retaining at least the safe dock margin. Training samples both types from the configured distribution. The threshold controller is solely a task feasibility probe.

Pilot begins with three seeds and 300,000 actions per condition. The last fixed-budget checkpoint is used for evaluation. Pilot observations may motivate a documented configuration revision. Formal work requires a separate, reviewed `formal` config, 20 declared seeds, and an immutable freeze bundle before training. No formal config or results are claimed here. Held-out data should be evaluated once after freeze. All model checkpoints, config hashes, episode rows, candidate rows, and compressed trajectories are retained.

For R1, a seed contributes the median of `PROD onset − RES onset` over matched original scenarios where both return events occurred. The prespecified minimum is 18 of 27 joint events per seed. A formal estimate is emitted only if all 20 declared seeds are present and estimable, there are no missing, duplicate, extra, or technical-error rows, and the phase is formal. The estimate is the median of seed medians, with a 10,000-resample seed bootstrap 95% interval and exact sign test. Absent events are never assigned an infinite onset. Completed-without-return, exhaustion before or after return, and administrative time limits retain separate counts. Event rate, work completion, and exhaustion accompany any timing contrast.
